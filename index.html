01

Alfie

AI-powered review operations — turning incoming customer reviews into validated, context-aware responses and business actions.

~$2M enterprise deal impact

The problem. Restaurants receive reviews continuously across Google, Yelp, Zomato, and other platforms. Responding well means understanding the review, the brand, the location, available offers or loyalty actions, business rules, language, and what should actually happen next. Doing that manually does not scale.

What I built. I led the architecture and end-to-end development of Alfie, a production AI workflow that turns each incoming review into a response-ready business event. Reviews are ingested from multiple platforms through scheduled jobs, normalized, placed onto a queue, and processed asynchronously by workers.

Each worker retrieves the appropriate brand context from cache. That context can include brand-specific guidance, offers, loyalty actions, business rules, and the information required for calculations or downstream actions.

If the review arrives in another language, the workflow can invoke a translation tool before sending the enriched context to the foundation model. The model generates the response, after which the output goes through a dedicated validation and guardrail layer before it can proceed.

The final workflow supports both operating modes: automatic execution when automation is enabled, or human review when an operator wants to inspect, edit, approve, and send the response themselves.

AI E2E Test Engineering

Verification for software increasingly written by AI.

Requirement → executable test AI-assisted verification workflow

The problem. As AI makes it dramatically easier to generate application code, verification becomes an increasingly important part of the engineering loop. Writing a test is not enough: the system needs to understand the requirement, understand the existing codebase, understand the application state, and make sure the required test data exists.

What I built. I built an AI-assisted E2E testing workflow around Playwright. A Jira or Notion ticket becomes the starting point. The system understands the requirement and codebase, uses a Playwright MCP server to explore the application, determines what state and data are required, and identifies missing prerequisites.

Once the environment is ready, the system produces the Playwright test specification. Tests can then run on a daily schedule or as part of a CI/CD pipeline.

Bulk Resolution

Making large customer-facing operations feel like a normal product action.

~100K tickets per operation

Customers needed to resolve or unresolve around 100,000 tickets in a single operation directly from the product. The existing implementation had grown into a collection of filters and separate ticket-type implementations, making large operations slow and difficult to reason about.

The important architectural decision was to separate selection from execution. Once the filters have been evaluated and the ticket IDs are known, the IDs are materialized in Redis under a deterministic key that represents the relevant account, user and filter combination.

The frontend receives that key. When the customer triggers the operation, the backend retrieves the materialized IDs and performs the resolve or unresolve operation without having to repeat the expensive selection work.

Restaurant Intelligence

Multi-platform analytics infrastructure built from scratch.

60s → <100ms query runtime after optimization

Restaurant feedback lived across multiple platforms including Google, Yelp, Zomato and others. I designed and built the analytics infrastructure from scratch to provide unified insights across brands, locations and individual outlets.

The system handled hundreds of restaurants, thousands of outlets and millions of records per week. I worked across ingestion, processing, storage and the application layer rather than treating analytics as a reporting-only problem.

One of the major performance problems was query latency approaching a minute. Through SQL optimization and intelligent indexing, frequently used analytical queries were reduced to under 100ms.

02 / Approach

How I work.

I care about the entire path from a problem someone has to a system people can rely on. Architecture matters, but so do adoption, operations and what happens after launch.

01

Start with the real problem

Understand who is affected, what they are doing today and where the actual bottleneck is before designing the system.

02

Separate expensive work

Move computation to the right boundary. Materialize expensive work when appropriate instead of repeatedly making the user wait.

03

AI is part of the system

Models need context, tools, constraints, validation and clear execution boundaries to become dependable production software.

04

Measure what happens

Usage analytics, RUM sessions, system metrics and customer behavior tell you whether the thing you built actually works.

05

Own the whole path

Architecture, implementation, deployment, customer interaction, debugging and adoption are all part of shipping software.

06

Design for adoption

A technically correct system has limited value if people cannot understand it, trust it or fit it into how they already work.
